2 Corinthians 1-3

1. What word appears in every verse of 2 Corinthians 1:3–7?

2. What is this word, and who gives it?

3. What does Paul boast about, what does he testify to, and what does he not rely on in conducting himself in the world, but instead rely on?

4. What is not “Yes” and “No,” but only what?

5. Who also put his seal of ownership on us and gave us what in our hearts as a deposit?

6. What word appears in every verse of 2 Corinthians 2:1–5 and 7?

7. What is this word, and what is its opposite?

8. What brings death, and what gives life?

9. “If what was transitory came with glory, how much greater is the glory of that which lasts?” What is “what was transitory,” and what is “that which lasts”?

---

2 Corinthians 4-6

1. “Meanwhile we groan, longing to be clothed instead with our heavenly dwelling.” What does the “tent” refer to here?

2. If anyone is in Christ, what is he or she?

3. The old has gone, and what has come?

4. What word appears in every verse of 2 Corinthians 5:18–20?

5. What is this word?

6. What kind of relationship is it, and between whom?

7. To whom was this ministry given?

8. To whom was this message entrusted?

9. “God made him who had no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God.” Who is “him who had no sin”?

10. What is the source of the words, “In the time of my favor I heard you, and in the day of salvation I helped you”?

11. Is now the time of God’s favor?

12. Is now the day of salvation?

13. Who should not be yoked together, be in agreement, or have anything in common?

14. What should not have fellowship, be in common, or be in agreement?

15. What is the source of the words, “I will live with them and walk among them, and I will be their God, and they will be my people”?

---

2 Corinthians 7-9

1. What are the three things Paul says they had not wronged, corrupted, or exploited anyone of, according to 2 Corinthians 7:2?

2. When Paul and his companions came into Macedonia, what did they have no rest from?

3. What were they facing on the outside and on the inside?

4. What kind of comfort did they receive that made them rejoice?

5. What are the two different kinds of sorrow in 2 Corinthians 7:10, and what does each result in?

6. Who was rich, yet for your sake became poor, so that you through his poverty might become rich?

7. What is the source of the words, “The one who gathered much did not have too much, and the one who gathered little did not have too little”?

8. What was being gathered, and what does it mean here?

9. Each person should give what he has decided in his heart to give, not reluctantly or under compulsion. Why?

10. What is the source of the words, “They have freely scattered their gifts to the poor; their righteousness endures forever”?

---

2 Corinthians 10-13

1. “The weapons we fight with are not the weapons of the world. On the contrary, they have divine power to demolish strongholds.” What can these weapons demolish?

2. What can they take captive?

3. “Let the one who boasts boast in the Lord.” Why?

4. “I am jealous for you with a godly jealousy.” Whose jealousy is this?

5. Why is Paul jealous for them?

6. How do the false apostles act?

7. Whose likeness do they disguise themselves as?

8. Paul was beaten by the Jews five times, with forty lashes minus one. Why was it not forty lashes?

9. “If I must boast, I will boast of the things that show my weakness.” What are the things that show his weakness?

10. According to 2 Corinthians 12:2–6, who was caught up to the third heaven and into Paradise?

11. What did Paul plead with the Lord three times about?

12. What did the Lord say to him?

13. By the time Paul wrote 2 Corinthians, how many times had he already visited Corinth?

14. What is the source of the words, “Every matter must be established by the testimony of two or three witnesses”?

15. To whom did Paul say, “If I come again, I will not spare those who sinned earlier”?

16. Why would he not spare them?

17. Who and what did Paul pray would be with them?
