Hebrews 1-3

1. Where do the quotations in Hebrews 1:5, 8–9, 10–12, and 13 come from, and why are they quoted?

2. “How shall we escape if we ignore so great a salvation?” What do you know about “so great a salvation”?

3. What did Jesus do so that he was crowned with glory and honor, was made perfect, destroyed the one who holds the power of death, and is able to help those who are being tempted?

4. Where do the quotations in Hebrews 2:6–8 and 12–13 come from?

5. How does Hebrews 3 compare Jesus with Moses?

6. Where does the quotation in Hebrews 3:7–11 come from?

7. What should believers be careful about so that none of them will have a sinful, unbelieving heart that turns away from the living God?

8. Hebrews 3 mentions “Today” several times. Do we still have a “Today”? Why?

9. Why were the people who came out of Egypt with Moses not able to enter God’s rest?

---

Hebrews 4-6

1. Why was the message they heard of no value to them?

2. Where does the quotation “On the seventh day God rested from all his works” come from?

3. What day was God speaking about when he said, “Today”?

4. What is the “Sabbath-rest” that still remains for the people of God?

5. The word of God is alive and active. What is it sharper than?

6. What does it penetrate, even to dividing what, and what does it judge?

7. Who is our great high priest who has ascended into heaven, and why is he able to empathize with our weaknesses?

8. How many high priests are mentioned in Hebrews 5?

9. Where does the quotation “You are a priest forever, in the order of Melchizedek” come from?

10. Who is the “you” in this verse?

11. Where does the quotation “I will surely bless you and give you many descendants” come from?

12. What did Abraham do before God swore by himself?

13. What are the “two unchangeable things” mentioned here?

---

Hebrews 7-9

1. Why was another priest needed, one in the order of Melchizedek rather than in the order of Aaron?

2. Who is he, and on what basis did he become a priest—not according to what, but according to what?

3. Where do the quotations “You are a priest forever, in the order of Melchizedek” and “The Lord has sworn and will not change his mind: ‘You are a priest forever’” come from?

4. How many times do these three chapters speak of something being “better”?

5. What are they?

6. Where does the quotation in Hebrews 8:8–12 come from?

7. Who is the mediator of the new covenant?

8. Since he died to set them free from what, what did those who are called receive?

9. Who was sacrificed once to take away the sins of many, and when he appears a second time to those who are waiting for him, what will he have nothing to do with, and what will he do instead?

---

Hebrews 10-13

1. The law is only a shadow of what is to come, not the realities themselves. Why can it never, by what it offers year after year, make perfect those who draw near to worship?

2. Where does the quotation in Hebrews 10:5–7 come from?

3. Since we have confidence to enter the Most Holy Place by what, and through whom has a new and living way been opened for us through the curtain?

4. What does the curtain represent?

5. If we deliberately keep on sinning after receiving the knowledge of the truth, what sacrifice is no longer left, but only what?

6. What is faith?

7. Hebrews 11 lists many people who were commended for their faith. Which three have influenced you the most?

8. These people were all commended for their faith, yet none of them received what had been promised. Why?

9. Who is the pioneer and perfecter of our faith?

10. What kind of discipline is painful rather than pleasant at the time, but later produces a harvest of righteousness and peace for whom?

11. For what price did Esau sell his birthright, and whose blessing did he lose as a result?

12. Where does the quotation “Once more I will shake not only the earth but also the heavens” come from?

13. What does “once more” indicate—that what can be shaken will be removed, so that what will remain?

14. What is it good for the heart to be strengthened by, rather than by what?

15. Why did Jesus suffer outside the city gate, making the people holy through his own blood?

16. Through Jesus, what kind of sacrifice should we continually offer to God—the sacrifice of what?

17. What should we not forget, because with such sacrifices God is pleased?

18. Who is the great Shepherd of the sheep?
